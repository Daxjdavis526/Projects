MODEL & ASSUMPTIONS

Spacetime

The gravitational field is an exact Kerr black hole in Cartesian Kerr–Schild coordinates (Schwarzschild when spin a = 0). Every pixel is a null geodesic integrated numerically on the GPU with a 4th-order Runge–Kutta scheme and adaptive step size. Gravitational lensing, the shadow, the photon ring and higher-order disk images emerge from this integration — nothing is painted in. Outside ~140 GM/c² light propagation is treated as straight (with an analytic weak-field deflection for rays that never enter that region).

Accretion disk

A geometrically thin, opaque equatorial disk between the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) and 24 r_g. Gas follows circular Kerr geodesics with Ω = (r^{3/2} + a)⁻¹. Each disk pixel is shaded with the relativistic factor g = ν_obs/ν_emit computed from the photon momentum and the local orbital four-velocity: Doppler boosting, relativistic beaming and gravitational redshift all come from this single quantity (intensity ∝ g³, color from a blackbody at g·T). The radial temperature profile follows a Shakura–Sunyaev-like law T ∝ r^{-3/4} with an ISCO cutoff.

Simplifications (honest list)

· Radiative transfer is simplified: an opaque blackbody proxy with procedural turbulence, no scattering, no light travel-time delay (the scene is a snapshot).
· Display temperatures are shifted into the visible band — a real disk at these accretion rates peaks in the UV/X-ray.
· Disk rotation is shown time-lapsed (real orbital periods near a 4×10⁶ M☉ black hole are tens of minutes to hours). Physics readouts always show true values.
· The camera is a stationary observer; navigation speed is a convenience and does not produce aberration or Doppler shift of the sky.
· Free fall uses the Schwarzschild radial-infall solution even when spin ≠ 0.
· The g-factor is clamped (0.05–3.2) to keep extreme beaming displayable.
· No interior physics is depicted: rays ending on the horizon render black.

Scale

1 simulation unit = 1 gravitational radius r_g = GM/c². All distances, times and tidal values in the interface are derived from the selected mass and spin. The visible shadow is larger than the horizon — its edge is set by photon capture, ≈ √27 r_g ≈ 5.2 r_g in radius for a=0.
