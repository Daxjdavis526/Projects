# Media verification log — cryogenic propulsion safety course

Working notes for every image considered for this course: what it shows, where it
came from, who is credited, what the publisher actually says about reuse, and how
confident I am. Nothing was downloaded whose licence I could not read on a page
published by the issuing organisation.

Verified 2026-09-08. All downloads live in `../img/`. The reader-facing credit
table is `../img/CREDITS.md`.

---

## 1. NASA media usage guidelines — read in full, summarised accurately

Source page: <https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/>
(section heading on that page: "NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines";
page last modified 2023-08-17 per its own metadata, re-read 2026-09-08).

NASA still images are **generally not subject to copyright in the United States**,
but "public domain" is not the whole story. NASA attaches conditions, and several
bite on a course like this one. Quoting the page:

**General permission**
> "NASA content – images, audio, video, and media files used in the rendition of
> 3-dimensional models, such as texture maps and polygon data in any format –
> generally are not subject to copyright in the United States. You may use this
> material for educational or informational purposes, including photo collections,
> textbooks, public exhibits, computer graphical simulations and Internet Web
> pages. This general permission extends to personal Web pages."

> "News outlets, schools, and text-book authors may use NASA content without
> needing explicit permission, subject to compliance with these guidelines. NASA
> content used in a factual manner that does not imply endorsement may be used
> without needing explicit permission. NASA should be acknowledged as the source
> of the material."

An educational course that uses these photographs factually, with NASA credited,
sits squarely inside that permission. It is a *permission*, not a licence: there
is no CC licence, no licence text, and no grant that survives being used in a way
the guidelines exclude.

**(a) No endorsement**
> "If the NASA material is to be used for commercial purposes, including
> advertisements, it must not explicitly or implicitly convey NASA's endorsement
> of commercial goods or services."

> "For use of NASA images clearances may be necessary for images that include any
> NASA logos or NASA employees to be used as cover art or in promotional content.
> Otherwise, NASA imagery can be generally used editorially within published works
> that are not promotional in nature."

Practical effect here: the course must not read as NASA-endorsed, NASA-approved or
NASA-accredited, and these photographs must not become cover art or marketing for
a paid product without clearance. `CREDITS.md` carries an explicit non-endorsement
statement for this reason.

**(b) Identifiable people**
> "If a NASA image, audio, video or media includes an identifiable person, using
> the media for commercial purposes may infringe that person's right of privacy or
> publicity, and permission should be obtained from the person."

> "Astronauts or employees who are currently employed by NASA cannot have their
> names, likenesses or other personality traits displayed or position title used
> on any commercial products, advertisements, promotional material or commercial
> product packaging."

Two downloaded images show identifiable NASA personnel
(`portable-liquid-cylinder-ln2-transfer-ksc.jpg` names the engineer in NASA's own
caption; `morpheus-lander-cryogenic-loading-slf.jpg` shows technicians at middle
distance). Both are flagged in the table below. Factual, educational use is fine;
neither may be used commercially or promotionally without the individual's
permission.

**(c) The NASA insignia**
> "The NASA Insignia, Logotype, identifiers, and imagery are not in the public
> domain. The use of the Insignia, Logotype and NASA identifiers is protected by
> law, and imagery is made available for use consistent with Media Usage
> Guidelines."

The meatball insignia appears *within* two downloaded photographs (on the
cryogenic road tanker in `morpheus-lander-cryogenic-loading-slf.jpg`, and on the
Orion stage adapter / mobile launcher in
`sls-cryogenic-propellant-loading-pad-39b.jpg`). Showing it as part of a
documentary photograph is ordinary editorial use. Cropping it out to use as a
logo, badge, favicon or course mark is **not** permitted and must not be done.

**(d) Third-party copyrighted material inside NASA collections**
> "NASA occasionally uses copyright-protected material of third parties with
> permission on its website. Those images will be marked identified as copyright
> protected with the name of the copyright holder. NASA's use does not convey any
> rights to others to use the same material. Those wishing to use copyright
> protected material of third parties must contact the copyright holder directly."

Every image below was checked for a third-party copyright marking in its NASA
metadata. None carried one; all are NASA-produced imagery from NASA centres.

**(e) Credit**
> "NASA should be acknowledged as the source of the material."

Where NASA publishes a photographer credit (e.g. "Photo credit: NASA/Kim
Shiflett") that exact string is reproduced in `CREDITS.md`. Where NASA's record
carries no photographer, the credit is plain "NASA" plus the originating centre.

**Two further conditions worth knowing** (not triggered by this course, recorded
so nobody trips over them later): NASA asks that its material not be used for NFTs
or cryptocurrency promotion; and for AI applications NASA states that "attribution
of the information directly to NASA is not permitted" in model output and that it
"does not permit our Insignia ... to appear with AI generated imagery or in the
training of AI tools."

NASA's `~orig` masters run 2–15 MB; every one here is the `~large` (1920 px) or

- **Source page:** <https://images.nasa.gov/details/KSC-20191108-PH-JBS01_0049>

- **Date:** 2019-11-08 · **Centre:** KSC
- **Confidence: high.** Photographer named in NASA's own metadata; subject
  unambiguous from NASA's caption.

- **Source page:** <https://images.nasa.gov/details/KSC-2014-4809>

  "Photo credit: NASA/Jim Grossmann"; the structured photographer field is empty)
- **Terms as published:** NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines, as above.
- **Date:** 2014-12-11 · **Centre:** KSC
- **Confidence: high** for provenance and credit; **medium** for my reading of the
  placard text, which is partly cut off at the frame edge — the visible fragments
  are "…AL GAS," / "…ED LIQUID" with UN number 1972, which is unambiguous, but say

- **Source page:** <https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/white-sands/ignition-susceptibility-and-flammability/>

- **Terms as published:** NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines, as above

## 5. Method notes

- Search was via the NASA Image and Video Library API (`images-api.nasa.gov`),
  which returns each asset's structured metadata — `photographer`,
  `secondary_creator`, `date_created`, `center`, `keywords` and the full caption.
  Credit lines below are taken from those fields and from the "Photo credit:"
  string NASA appends to many captions, not inferred.
- Every image was opened and looked at before selection. Three otherwise-plausible
  candidates were dropped purely because the photograph did not show what its
  caption implied.
- Variant selection: NASA publishes `~orig`, `~large` (1920 px), `~medium`
  (1280 px), `~small` and `~thumb`. `~large` was taken wherever it exists and
  lands under ~600 KB; `~medium` where it does not. No image here needed
  re-encoding.
