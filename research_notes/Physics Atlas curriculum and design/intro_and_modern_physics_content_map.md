## 1. Course identity, USU sequencing, and which course each topic belongs to

### Takeaway
USU runs two parallel calculus-based introductory lecture tracks, each 4 credits:
- PHYS 2210 and PHYS 2220, "Physics for Scientists and Engineers I/II", with a required recitation;
- PHYS 2310 and PHYS 2320, "Physics for Scientists I/II".

The labs are the separate 1-credit, computer-assisted courses PHYS 2215 and PHYS 2225. PHYS 2310 and 2320 are therefore not labs. "2210/2310" means two alternative lecture versions of Physics I, and "2220/2320" two versions of Physics II.

Where the topics fall in USU's catalog:
- Physics I: mechanics, harmonic motion, gravity, fluids and thermodynamics.
- Physics II: waves and sound, electricity and magnetism, DC/AC circuits, Maxwell's equations, optics, special relativity, wave–particle duality, and atomic, molecular and nuclear phenomena.
- PHYS 2710: relativity and quantum mechanics, applied to atomic, solid-state and particle physics.
- PHYS 3710, as taught by David Peak: special relativity, general relativity, and nuclear and sub-nuclear structure, including Feynman diagrams for quantum electrodynamics (QED).

## 2. Introductory Physics I (PHYS 2210 or PHYS 2310, lab PHYS 2215)

### Takeaway
At USU, Physics I is mechanics plus harmonic motion, gravitation, fluids and thermodynamics. That corresponds to OpenStax Volume 1 chapters 1–15 plus Volume 2 chapters 1–4. Waves and sound (OS1 ch16–17) move to Physics II.

The best-documented misconceptions are in Newtonian force reasoning (the Force Concept Inventory taxonomy) and in reading kinematics graphs (the TUG-K test). These should seed the Atlas's multiple-choice distractors and identify-the-error items.

For this learner, the GR seeds to plant are: inertial frames, the equivalence of inertial and gravitational mass, tides, the effective potential, and escape speed versus the Schwarzschild radius. The aerospace hooks are rocket propulsion, orbits, drag and Bernoulli flow.

### Cited Findings
**Scope and textbook structure**
- USU Physics I scope is given in Section 1 (PHYS 2210/2310 catalog lists; PHYS 2215 lab). — [USU catalog PHYS 2210](https://catalog.usu.edu/courses/PHYS2210); [USU catalog PHYS 2310](https://catalog.usu.edu/courses/PHYS2310); [USU catalog PHYS 2215](https://catalog.usu.edu/courses/PHYS2215)
- **OpenStax University Physics Vol 1 structure.** Unit 1, Mechanics, runs from ch1 Units and Measurement, ch2 Vectors, ch3 Motion Along a Straight Line and ch4 Motion in Two and Three Dimensions through Newton's laws, work and kinetic energy, momentum, rotation and gravitation, ending at ch14 Fluid Mechanics. Unit 2, Waves and Acoustics, is ch15 Oscillations, ch16 Waves and ch17 Sound. — [OpenStax UP Vol 1 preface](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/preface); [UCF Pressbooks mirror of Vol 1](https://pressbooks.online.ucf.edu/osuniversityphysics/); [Vol 1 PDF](https://d3bxy9euw4e147.cloudfront.net/oscms-prodcms/media/documents/UniversityPhysicsVolume1-LR.pdf)
- OpenStax Vol 2 covers thermodynamics and electricity and magnetism, so temperature and heat are Volume 2 content. — [OpenStax UP Vol 2 index](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/index); [Vol 2 PDF](https://assets.openstax.org/oscms-prodcms/media/documents/University_Physics_Volume_2_-_WEB.pdf)

**Misconceptions documented by physics education research**
- **Force Concept Inventory (FCI).** Hestenes, Wells and Swackhamer, *The Physics Teacher* 30, 141–158 (1992), include Table II, "A Taxonomy of Misconceptions Probed by the Inventory". A misconception is indicated when a student selects the corresponding item. Examples:
  - kinematics: "position–velocity undiscriminated";
  - force reasoning: "greater mass implies greater force" and "most active agent produces greatest force";
  - the "dominance principle" is so natural to students that it is among the last ideas to disappear.
  The taxonomy came from qualitative interview studies and was later refined by Hestenes and Jackson. — [ResearchGate: Force Concept Inventory](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/216743208_Force_Concept_Inventory); [Modified module analysis of the FCI (arXiv 1905.06176)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.06176); [ComPADRE FCI record](https://www.compadre.org/portal/services/detail.cfm?ID=2641)
- **Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation (FMCE).** It probes only a limited number of the misconceptions the FCI authors originally outlined. — [FMCE modified module analysis (arXiv 2001.00967)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.00967)
- **Test of Understanding Graphs in Kinematics (TUG-K).** Beichner, "Testing student interpretation of kinematics graphs", *AJP* 62(8), 750 (1994), studied 895 high-school and college students. Documented difficulties:
  - reading a graph as a picture of the motion;
  - slope/height confusion;
  - finding the slope of a line that does not pass through the origin;
  - interpreting the area under a curve;
  - confusing the variables.
  Planinić et al. (2012) found slope/height confusion in both physics and math contexts, but much more often in physics, and concluded that a lack of math knowledge is not the main cause. — [Beichner 1994 PDF](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/732257/mod_resource/content/1/Testing%20student%20understanding%20kinematics%20graphs.pdf); [Planinić et al. 2012](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/418623/mod_resource/content/1/Planinic2012_Article_COMPARISONOFSTUDENTUNDERSTANDI.pdf); [TA pedagogical content knowledge on the TUG-K (arXiv 1307.7179)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.7179); [ComPADRE TUG-K](https://www.compadre.org/portal/items/detail.cfm?ID=2390)
- **Energy and Momentum Conceptual Survey (EMCS).** An established instrument that has been used to track how understanding progresses from introductory to advanced levels. — [EMCS progression study (arXiv 2311.17054)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17054)

**Lab standards**
- **AAPT 2014 lab recommendations.** Six focus areas: constructing knowledge, modeling, designing experiments, developing technical and practical lab skills, analyzing and visualizing data, and communicating physics. None of the outcomes target conceptual understanding of physics content. "Modeling" means treating models as abstract representations of physical and measurement systems, with limitations and approximations, and developing, evaluating and testing them. "Designing experiments" means developing, evaluating and troubleshooting experiments that test models. — [Operationalizing the AAPT lab goals (arXiv 1809.00059)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00059); [AAPT Lab Guidelines document](https://cas-web-lp001.phy.ohio.edu/~rochej/advanced_lab_doc/LabGuidlinesDocument_EBendorsed_nov10.pdf)
- Research-based lab practice treats labs as a place to teach experimentation and critical thinking, and an instrument exists to measure that (the PLIC). — [Holmes, AAPT New Faculty slides](https://aapt.org/Conferences/newfaculty/upload/Holmes_Using-physics-labs-to-teach-experimentation-and-critical-thinking.pdf); [Physics Lab Inventory of Critical thinking (Cornell)](https://cperl.lassp.cornell.edu/sites/cperl/files/publications/170719_PLIC.pdf)

### Inferences
#### (2) Major subjects
1. Measurement and vectors.
2. 1D and 2D kinematics.
3. Newtonian dynamics and its applications: friction, inclines, connected objects, circular motion, drag, non-inertial frames.
4. Work and energy.
5. Momentum, collisions and center of mass, including rocket propulsion.
6. Rotation, torque, angular momentum and rolling.
7. Static equilibrium and an introduction to elasticity.
8. Gravitation and orbits.
9. Fluid statics and dynamics.
10. Oscillations.
11. Temperature, heat and kinetic theory.
12. The first and second laws of thermodynamics.

#### (3) Module → submodule → lesson tree
Sources reconciled: the USU 2210/2310 catalog lists, OpenStax Vol 1 ch1–15 and Vol 2 ch1–4, and standard Young & Freedman, Halliday/Resnick/Walker and Knight sequencing (from domain knowledge; see Gaps).

**M1 Measurement, units and estimation [C] (OS1 ch1)**
- 1.1 SI base units, prefixes, unit conversion chains; reading aerospace units (ft, lbf, slug, psi, knots) [AERO]
- 1.2 Dimensional analysis: checking equations; deriving scaling laws, e.g. T ∝ √(L/g)
- 1.3 Orders of magnitude and Fermi estimates
- 1.4 Significant figures, uncertainty, reporting a result with its uncertainty [LAB]
- 1.5 A problem-solving framework: model → visualize → solve → assess

**M2 Vectors [C] (OS1 ch2)**
- 2.1 Scalars versus vectors, with displacement as the prototype vector
- 2.2 Components and unit vectors î, ĵ, k̂; magnitude and direction; polar ↔ Cartesian
- 2.3 Adding and subtracting vectors, graphically and by components
- 2.4 The dot product as projection (preview of work and flux)
- 2.5 The cross product and the right-hand rule (preview of torque, L and magnetic force)
- 2.6 [E][GR] A vector is frame-independent; its components are not. Rotating the axes changes the components but not the arrow. This is the seed of four-vectors and tensors.

**M3 Motion along a line [C] (OS1 ch3)**
- 3.1 Position, displacement and distance; average velocity versus average speed
- 3.2 Instantaneous velocity as a derivative: the slope of x(t)
- 3.3 Acceleration as the slope of v(t); the sign of a versus speeding up or slowing down
- 3.4 The constant-acceleration equations, and when they are valid
- 3.5 Free fall: a = −g throughout, including at the top of the motion
- 3.6 Calculus kinematics: v = ∫a dt and x = ∫v dt; area under a graph; non-constant a (e.g. a = −kv)
- 3.7 Translating between x–t, v–t and a–t graphs

**M4 Motion in 2D and 3D [C] (OS1 ch4)**
- 4.1 Position, velocity and acceleration vectors; the components evolve independently
- 4.2 Projectile motion: time of flight, range, maximum height, launch from a height
- 4.3 The trajectory equation; why 45° is optimal only for level ground with no drag
- 4.4 Uniform circular motion: a_c = v²/r = ω²r; period and frequency
- 4.5 Non-uniform circular motion: tangential plus radial acceleration
- 4.6 Relative motion and Galilean velocity addition (river crossing, aircraft in a crosswind) [AERO]
- 4.7 [GR] Acceleration is the same in every inertial frame. This defines inertial frames and sets up the special-relativity postulates.

**M5 Newton's laws [C] (OS1 ch5)**
- 5.1 Force as an interaction; contact versus long-range forces; net force
- 5.2 First law and inertial reference frames [GR]
- 5.3 Second law in vector form; writing component equations
- 5.4 Mass versus weight; apparent weight in an elevator [GR: preview of the equivalence principle]
- 5.5 Third law: interaction pairs act on different objects
- 5.6 Common forces: weight, normal force, ideal tension, spring force (Hooke's law)
- 5.7 Free-body diagrams: choosing the system; internal versus external forces

**M6 Applications of Newton's laws [C] (OS1 ch6)**
- 6.1 Equilibrium: hanging masses, tensions at angles
- 6.2 Friction: static (f_s ≤ μ_s N) versus kinetic (f_k = μ_k N)
- 6.3 Inclines: tilted axes; the angle of repose, tan θ = μ_s
- 6.4 Connected objects: the Atwood machine, a table block pulled by a hanging mass, constraint equations
- 6.5 Circular dynamics: flat and banked curves, the conical pendulum, vertical loops (v_min = √(gR) at the top), an orbit as free fall
- 6.6 Drag and terminal speed: linear and quadratic drag, v_t = √(2mg/(ρC_D A)) [AERO]
- 6.7 [E][GR] Non-inertial frames and fictitious forces (centrifugal, Coriolis); the accelerating elevator

**M7 Work and kinetic energy [C] (OS1 ch7)**
- 7.1 Work by a constant force (dot product, sign of work)
- 7.2 Work by a variable force: ∫F·dr, area under F–x, spring work ½kx²
- 7.3 Kinetic energy and the work–energy theorem
- 7.4 Power, P = F·v; thrust power [AERO]

**M8 Potential energy and energy conservation [C] (OS1 ch8)**
- 8.1 Conservative versus non-conservative forces (path independence)
- 8.2 Potential-energy functions; F = −dU/dx
- 8.3 Conservation of mechanical energy; choosing the zero of U
- 8.4 Energy diagrams: turning points; stable, unstable and neutral equilibrium [GR: preview of the effective potential]
- 8.5 Friction and thermal energy; energy bookkeeping with bar charts; the choice of system
- 8.6 [E] Energy sources and efficiency

**M9 Momentum, collisions and center of mass [C] (OS1 ch9)**
- 9.1 Impulse–momentum: J = ∫F dt; landing gear and airbags
- 9.2 Conservation of momentum for an isolated system
- 9.3 1D collisions: elastic, inelastic, perfectly inelastic; KE accounting
- 9.4 2D collisions in components; equal-mass elastic glancing collisions give a 90° opening angle
- 9.5 Center of mass of discrete and continuous systems (∫x dm with λ = dm/dx)
- 9.6 Motion of the center of mass; the center-of-mass frame [GR: the zero-momentum frame becomes invariant mass in 3710]
- 9.7 Rocket propulsion: thrust = ṁv_e; Δv = v_e ln(m₀/m_f); staging [AERO] [C for this learner]

**M10 Fixed-axis rotation [C] (OS1 ch10)**
- 10.1 Angular position, velocity and acceleration (axial vectors)
- 10.2 Constant-α kinematics
- 10.3 Linking angular and linear quantities: v = rω, a_t = rα, a_c = ω²r
- 10.4 Rotational kinetic energy and moment of inertia
- 10.5 Computing I by integration (rod, ring, disk, sphere); the parallel-axis theorem
- 10.6 Torque τ = r × F; the lever arm
- 10.7 Στ = Iα; pulleys with mass
- 10.8 Rotational work and power (W = ∫τ dθ, P = τω)

**M11 Angular momentum and rolling [C] (OS1 ch11)**
- 11.1 Rolling without slipping: v_cm = Rω; how energy is shared; a shape race down an incline
- 11.2 Angular momentum: L = r × p and L = Iω
- 11.3 Conservation of L: skater, collapsing star [GR], Kepler's second law
- 11.4 Gyroscope precession, Ω = τ/L; reaction wheels and spin stabilization [AERO]

**M12 Static equilibrium and elasticity [C/E] (OS1 ch12)**
- 12.1 Equilibrium conditions: ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0 about any point; center of gravity
- 12.2 Ladders, beams and cranes
- 12.3 [E] Stress, strain, Young's/bulk/shear moduli; elastic versus plastic behaviour [AERO: structures]

**M13 Gravitation [C][GR] (OS1 ch13)**
- 13.1 The universal law of gravitation; G and the Cavendish experiment
- 13.2 g(r) near Earth; the shell theorem (qualitative); Earth's rotation and apparent weight
- 13.3 U = −GMm/r; total energy; escape speed
- 13.4 Circular orbits and their energy (E = −GMm/2r); LEO and GEO [AERO]
- 13.5 Kepler's laws (T² ∝ a³); [E][AERO] Hohmann transfer
- 13.6 Tidal forces (differential gravity; the Roche limit qualitatively) [GR: tides are curvature]
- 13.7 [E][GR] Preview of Einstein's gravity: the equivalence principle, light bending, and r_s = 2GM/c² (the escape speed equals c there)

**M14 Fluid mechanics [C] (OS1 ch14)**
- 14.1 Density and pressure; pressure acts normal to any surface
- 14.2 P = P₀ + ρgh; gauge versus absolute pressure; the atmosphere and altimetry [AERO]
- 14.3 Manometers and barometers
- 14.4 Pascal's principle and hydraulics
- 14.5 Archimedes' principle; floating fraction; balloons [AERO]
- 14.6 Flow rate and continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
- 14.7 Bernoulli's equation; Pitot tubes and Venturi meters [AERO]
- 14.8 [E] Viscosity, Poiseuille flow, the Reynolds number and turbulence

**M15 Oscillations [C] (OS1 ch15)**
- 15.1 SHM: x = A cos(ωt + φ) with ω = √(k/m); A and φ from the initial conditions
- 15.2 Energy in SHM
- 15.3 The reference circle: SHM as the projection of uniform circular motion (preview of phasors)
- 15.4 Simple, physical and torsional pendulums; the small-angle approximation
- 15.5 Damped oscillation: under-, critically and over-damped
- 15.6 Driven oscillation, resonance and Q [AERO: vibration]
- 15.7 [E][GR] Near any potential minimum, small oscillations are SHM (Taylor expansion of U). Leads to normal modes, the quantum oscillator in 2710, and the free test masses in a gravitational-wave detector.

**M16 Temperature and heat [C] (OS2 ch1)**
- 16.1 Temperature, thermal equilibrium, the zeroth law
- 16.2 Thermometers and the K, °C and °F scales; absolute zero
- 16.3 Thermal expansion and thermal stress
- 16.4 Heat, specific heat, calorimetry
- 16.5 Phase changes and latent heat; phase diagrams
- 16.6 Conduction (Fourier's law), convection, and radiation (σT⁴) [AERO: spacecraft thermal control and re-entry]

**M17 Kinetic theory of gases [C] (OS2 ch2)**
- 17.1 The ideal-gas law, PV = nRT = NkT
- 17.2 Pressure from molecular impulses; T ∝ average translational KE; v_rms
- 17.3 Equipartition and heat capacity, C_V = (f/2)R
- 17.4 [E] The Maxwell–Boltzmann speed distribution (feeds 2710 statistical physics)

**M18 First law of thermodynamics [C] (OS2 ch3)**
- 18.1 Systems, state variables, quasi-static processes
- 18.2 Work by a gas, W = ∫P dV, as the area on a PV diagram; heat; internal energy

#### (6) Foundational concepts that short catalog descriptions leave implicit
- **Models and idealizations:** point particle, massless inextensible string, ideal pulley, frictionless surface, rigid body, incompressible fluid, ideal gas. Each sim should say which idealizations it uses.
- **Choosing a system and its boundary**, which decides which forces are internal and which are external, and which energy and momentum transfers count.
- **Interaction pairs (third law) versus balanced forces on one object.**
- **Inertial frames and Galilean invariance** [GR]: the laws are the same in all inertial frames; velocity is relative, acceleration is not.
- **Inertial mass equals gravitational mass** [GR]: all bodies fall alike in vacuum; apparent weightlessness in free fall.
- **Fictitious forces** appear only in accelerating frames [GR: gravity as a fictitious force is the 3710 punchline].
- **Conservation laws as consequences of symmetry** [E]: time symmetry gives energy, translation gives momentum, rotation gives angular momentum (Noether). In GR these become conserved quantities along geodesics.
- **Energy diagrams as phase-space intuition:** turning points, bound versus unbound motion.
- **Linearization near equilibrium**, so that almost everything oscillates harmonically for small amplitudes.
- **Dimensional analysis and limiting-case checks** as habitual "assess" steps.
- **Uncertainty as part of a measured value**, not a mistake.
- **Pressure as a scalar at a point**, with the force it exerts on any surface acting normal to that surface.
- **Heat and work as processes**; temperature and internal energy as state properties.

#### (7) Problem-solving techniques and representative archetypes
Techniques:
- Draw first: a free-body diagram, a motion diagram, or energy bar charts.
- Pick tilted axes along an incline.
- Write one equation per object per axis, plus constraint equations.
- Choose the tool to fit the question: Newton's laws for forces and time; energy for speed and position; momentum for collisions and explosions; angular momentum for spin-up and spin-down.
- Check symmetry and limiting cases (m₂ → 0, θ → 0, μ → 0).
- Do a dimensional check and a Fermi-style sanity check.

#### (9) Connections to advanced physics and GR
- **Inertial frames and Galilean relativity (M4–M5)** lead to the special-relativity postulates.
- **The elevator, apparent weight and free-fall "weightlessness" (M5–M6)** lead to the equivalence principle.
- **Fictitious forces** lead to gravity as an inertial (fictitious) effect in GR.
- **Tides (M13.6)** lead to geodesic deviation and spacetime curvature.
- **Energy diagrams (M8.4) and the orbital effective potential (S13)** lead to Schwarzschild effective potentials, perihelion precession and the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO).
- **Escape speed √(2GM/R) set equal to c (M13.7)** gives r_s = 2GM/c². The Newtonian derivation happens to give the right formula, which makes a good "why this works only by coincidence" enrichment.
- **Noether symmetries** lead to energy and angular momentum conserved along geodesics.
- **The center-of-mass frame** leads to the zero-momentum frame and invariant mass.
- **SHM and resonance** lead to quantum oscillators, normal modes and gravitational-wave detector physics.
- **The rocket equation** leads to the relativistic rocket (rapidity = (v_e/c) ln(m₀/m_f)) in 3710.
- **Gyroscopes** lead to geodetic and frame-dragging precession (Gravity Probe B, enrichment).
- **Fluids and pressure** lead to hydrostatic equilibrium in stars and the perfect-fluid source of gravity in cosmology.
- **Entropy** leads to black-hole thermodynamics (enrichment).

#### (10) Depth and rigor
This is calculus-based but not Lagrangian:
- single-variable integrals for work, center of mass and moment of inertia, for uniform or simply varying densities;
- ODEs solved by inspection (SHM, linear drag);

#### (11) Distractor and identify-the-error bank
Each item gives the misconception, then a one-sentence micro-teaching line.

PER-documented (see Cited Findings):
- **Position–velocity undiscriminated** (FCI): "two objects side by side have equal speeds." *Micro-teaching:* "Being at the same place at the same moment says nothing about speed; compare slopes, not positions."
- **Greater mass implies greater force** and **most active agent produces greatest force** (FCI): "the truck hits the car harder." *Micro-teaching:* "In any interaction the two forces are equal and opposite; the lighter object just accelerates more."
- **Dominance principle** (FCI). *Micro-teaching:* the same third-law line, paired with a force-sensor collision animation.
- **Slope/height confusion and graph-as-picture** (TUG-K). *Micro-teaching:* "Velocity is the slope of the position graph, not its height; displacement is the area under the velocity graph."
- **Slope of a line not through the origin** (TUG-K), e.g. computing x/t instead of Δx/Δt. *Micro-teaching:* "A slope is a ratio of changes; use two points on the line, not one point and the origin."

Design-inferred distractors. These are standard error patterns, but their PER sources were not verified in this session; see Gaps.
- **Free fall:** "a = 0 (or v = 0) at the top." *Micro-teaching:* "Gravity never switches off; at the top only the vertical velocity is zero."
- **Normal force:** "N always equals mg" (incline, elevator, loop). *Micro-teaching:* "N is whatever the surface must push to stop you sinking in; find it from ΣF = ma."
- **Static friction:** "it always equals μ_s N." *Micro-teaching:* "Static friction matches the need, up to a maximum of μ_s N."
- **Atwood tension:** "T equals the hanging weight." *Micro-teaching:* "If the hanging mass accelerates downward, the string must pull up less than its weight."
- **Circular release:** "released from circular motion, it flies outward radially." *Micro-teaching:* "With the inward force gone, it continues along the tangent."
- **"Centrifugal force acts on the car"** (in the ground frame). *Micro-teaching:* "In the ground frame nothing pushes outward; friction pulls inward, and your body tends to go straight."
- **"No gravity on the ISS."** *Micro-teaching:* at 400 km, g is about 88% of its surface value, (6371/6771)² ≈ 0.885, so "astronauts float because they and the station fall together."
- **"Satellites need constant thrust to stay up."** *Micro-teaching:* "An orbit is free fall with enough sideways speed to keep missing the planet."
- **"Momentum isn't conserved in inelastic collisions."** *Micro-teaching:* "Kinetic energy is lost, but momentum is conserved whenever the external impulse is negligible."
- **"Heavier balls win the rolling race."** *Micro-teaching:* "Only the shape factor I/MR² matters."
- **"A larger force always gives a larger torque."** *Micro-teaching:* "Torque is the force times its lever arm; a large force through the axis gives zero torque."
- **"Pressure is larger in the wider tank at the same depth."** *Micro-teaching:* "Pressure depends on depth and density only."
- **"Buoyant force grows with depth."** *Micro-teaching:* "For an incompressible fluid, the buoyant force equals the weight of the displaced fluid at any depth."
- **The "equal transit time" explanation of lift.** *Micro-teaching:* "Air over the top of a wing actually arrives at the trailing edge earlier; lift comes from the wing turning the flow."
- **"A pendulum's period depends on its mass or (small) amplitude."** *Micro-teaching:* "For small swings, T = 2π√(L/g), and nothing else matters."
- **"Heat and temperature are the same thing"** and **"metal feels colder, so it is colder."** *Micro-teaching:* "Temperature is a state; heat is energy in transit; metal conducts heat away from your hand faster."
- **"No heat flows in an adiabatic compression, so the temperature cannot change."** *Micro-teaching:* "Work done on the gas raises its internal energy, and so its temperature."
- **"A frictionless engine can reach 100% efficiency."** *Micro-teaching:* "The second law caps efficiency at 1 − T_c/T_h even with no friction at all."
