## Key question 1 — Course identity, and how the Griffiths chapters split between E&M I and II (all four courses)

### Takeaway
USU's current catalog wording maps PHYS 3600 onto Griffiths ch. 1–6 (statics plus materials) and PHYS 4600 onto ch. 7–12 (induction through relativistic electrodynamics). That is the most common national split. The variants are 1–5 | 6–12, and E&M II courses that cut parts of ch. 8 and 11. Third-party listings describe PHYS 4700 (QM I) as reaching angular momentum, spin and perturbation theory. No official USU description of PHYS 4710 (QM II) was found, and listings disagree on the numbering.

### Inferences
- **Mapping USU onto Griffiths E&M.**
  - PHYS 3600 ≈ Griffiths ch. 1–6. "Response of materials ... to electric and magnetic fields" brings in ch. 4 and 6. Expect "the Maxwell equations" there to mean the static div/curl equations, with at most a preview of the full set.

## Key question 2 — E&M I (PHYS 3600): major subjects, module tree, math skills, prerequisites, hidden foundations

### Takeaway
E&M I is the static half of field theory. It covers the vector-calculus toolkit and Dirac delta, then electrostatics, potentials and energy, conductors, and boundary-value techniques (images, separation of variables with Legendre polynomials, multipoles). It finishes with dielectrics, magnetostatics, the vector potential and magnetic materials. Its real hurdle is mathematical, not conceptual. PER shows junior-level difficulties cluster around the delta function, curvilinear vector calculus, Gauss's-law symmetry arguments and separation of variables.

### Cited Findings
- USU catalog topics for PHYS 3600: electrostatics; magnetostatics; the response of conductors and dielectrics to electric and magnetic fields; the Maxwell equations; Coulomb's and Biot–Savart laws; scalar and vector potentials; boundary conditions; multipole expansions; energy. — [USU catalog, PHYS 3600](https://usu.catalog.acalog.com/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=39&coid=330709)
- Griffiths ch. 1 opens with 1.1 Vector Algebra, 1.2 Differential Calculus and 1.3 Integral Calculus, in a TOC with numbered subsections. — [Cambridge TOC PDF](https://assets.cambridge.org/97810093/97759/toc/9781009397759_toc.pdf). Appendices cover vector calculus in curvilinear coordinates, the Helmholtz theorem, and units. — [BRAC library TOC](https://library.bracu.ac.bd/vufind/Record/46397/TOC)
- **Zangwill's ordering (graduate-leaning reference).** 1 Mathematical preliminaries, 2 The Maxwell equations, 3 Electrostatics, 4 Electric multipoles, 5 Conducting matter, 6 Dielectric matter, 7 Laplace's equation, 8 Poisson's equation, 9 Steady current, 10 Magnetostatics, 11 Magnetic multipoles, 12 Magnetic force and energy, 13 Magnetic matter. ### Inferences
**Module → submodule → subtopic tree for E&M I.** [G x.y] means Griffiths 4th-ed. section; [Z n] means Zangwill chapter. ★ = core, ◇ = enrichment.

- **M0 Vector-calculus and distribution toolkit** [G 1; Z 1; App. A, B] ★
  - 0.1 Vector algebra: dot and cross products; triple products; the separation vector 𝓇 = r − r′ ("script-r": the arrow from source point r′ to field point r); how components transform under rotation. ◇ index notation with ε_ijk (Levi-Civita symbol, the fully antisymmetric "sign of the permutation" symbol) as an early on-ramp to tensors.
  - 0.2 Differential calculus: ∇ ("del", the vector of partial derivatives).
    - Gradient = steepest-ascent direction and rate.
    - Divergence = net outflow per unit volume.
    - Curl = circulation per unit area.
    - Product rules; ∇² (the Laplacian, "divergence of the gradient"); the identities ∇×∇f = 0 and ∇·(∇×A) = 0.
  - 0.3 Integral calculus: line, surface and volume integrals; the fundamental theorem for gradients; the divergence (Gauss) theorem; Stokes' theorem; integration by parts with boundary terms.
  - 0.4 Curvilinear coordinates (spherical r, θ, φ and cylindrical s, φ, z).
    - Unit vectors change with position (r̂ cannot be pulled out of an integral).
    - Line, area and volume elements.
    - Div, curl and Laplacian formulas.
  - 0.5 Dirac delta.
    - 1D sifting property; δ(kx) = δ(x)/|k|; δ carries units of 1/[argument].
    - 3D δ³(r), units 1/length³.
    - The key identity ∇·(r̂/r²) = 4πδ³(r).
    - Shells and lines in curvilinear form: σ δ(r − R); λ δ(s)/(2πs).
  - 0.6 Theory of vector fields: Helmholtz theorem (a field that vanishes at infinity is fixed by its divergence and curl); curl-free ⇒ gradient of a scalar; divergence-free ⇒ curl of a vector.
- **M1 The electrostatic field** [G 2.1–2.2; Z 3] ★
  - 1.1 Coulomb's law, ε₀ (vacuum permittivity), superposition.
  - 1.2 Continuous distributions λ, σ, ρ (line, surface and volume charge densities).
  - 1.3 Field lines, flux, Gauss's law in integral and differential form (∇·E = ρ/ε₀, derived from the δ identity).
  - 1.4 Gauss's-law applications: spherical, cylindrical and planar symmetry; the symmetry argument that has to come first; cases where Gauss's law is true but useless.
  - 1.5 ∇×E = 0 for static fields.
- **M2 Electric potential and electrostatic energy** [G 2.3–2.4] ★
  - 2.1 V is defined up to a constant (choice of reference point); path independence; E = −∇V; V of localized distributions.
  - 2.2 Poisson ∇²V = −ρ/ε₀ and Laplace ∇²V = 0.
  - 2.3 Boundary conditions: E⊥ jumps by σ/ε₀, E∥ is continuous, V is continuous.
  - 2.4 Work and energy: W = ½Σq_iV_i; W = ½∫ρV dτ = (ε₀/2)∫E² dτ (dτ = volume element). Energy is quadratic, so it does not superpose. The self-energy of a point charge is infinite.
- **M3 Conductors and capacitors** [G 2.5; Z 5] ★
  - 3.1 Conductor properties: E = 0 and ρ = 0 inside; equipotential; charge on the surface; E ⊥ surface just outside.
  - 3.2 Induced charges, cavities, shielding.
  - 3.3 Surface force: pressure = σ²/2ε₀ (uses the average of the inside and outside field).
  - 3.4 Capacitance C = Q/V for plate, sphere and cylinder geometries; energy ½CV².
- **M4 Boundary-value techniques** [G 3; Z 4, 7, 8] ★
  - 4.1 Properties of Laplace's equation: mean-value property, no interior extrema, Earnshaw's theorem.
  - 4.2 First and second uniqueness theorems: why guessing a solution is legitimate.
  - 4.3 Method of images: point charge plus grounded plane (induced σ, force, energy, including why the energy is half the image system's); point charge plus grounded sphere.
  - 4.4 Separation of variables.
    - Cartesian: slots and boxes; Fourier series; "Fourier's trick", which uses orthogonality to pick off coefficients.
    - Spherical with azimuthal symmetry: Legendre polynomials P_l(cos θ); general solution Σ(A_l r^l + B_l r^{−l−1})P_l; conducting sphere in a uniform field; sphere with prescribed V(θ) or σ(θ).
    - ◇ Cylindrical solutions / Bessel functions.
  - 4.5 Multipole expansion: 1/𝓇 = Σ (r′^l / r^{l+1}) P_l(cos α); monopole, dipole and quadrupole terms; the dipole moment's origin dependence; coordinate-free field of a pure dipole; dipole in an external field (torque p×E, energy −p·E).
- **M5 Electric fields in matter** [G 4; Z 6] ★
  - 5.1 Polarization: atomic polarizability α (induced p = αE); polar-molecule alignment; P = dipole moment per unit volume.
  - 5.2 Bound charges σ_b = P·n̂ and ρ_b = −∇·P; their physical picture; the macroscopic field inside matter.
  - 5.3 D = ε₀E + P with ∇·D = ρ_f. The "deceptive parallel": ∇×D ≠ 0 in general, so D is *not* fixed by free charge alone. Boundary conditions on D and E.
  - 5.4 Linear dielectrics.
    - χ_e, ε and ε_r (susceptibility, permittivity, dielectric constant).
    - Boundary-value problems: dielectric sphere in a uniform field; partially filled capacitors.
    - Energy W = ½∫D·E dτ.
    - Force pulling a slab into a capacitor (fringing field).
  - 5.5 ◇ Clausius–Mossotti relation; ferroelectrics and piezoelectrics.
- **M6 Magnetostatics** [G 5; Z 9–12] ★
  - 6.1 Lorentz force F = q(E + v×B); magnetic forces do no work; cyclotron and cycloid motion.
  - 6.2 Currents I, K, J (line, surface, volume); continuity ∇·J = −∂ρ/∂t; steady currents.
  - 6.3 Biot–Savart law: segments, loops, on-axis solenoid; force between parallel wires.
  - 6.4 ∇·B = 0 and ∇×B = μ₀J (μ₀ = vacuum permeability). Ampère's law for wire, sheet, solenoid and toroid. Side-by-side comparison with electrostatics.
  - 6.5 Vector potential A with B = ∇×A.
    - Coulomb gauge ∇·A = 0, giving ∇²A = −μ₀J.
    - A of a solenoid circulates like the current.
    - Boundary conditions: B⊥ continuous; B∥ jumps by μ₀K×n̂; A continuous.
  - 6.6 Multipole expansion of A: no monopole term; magnetic dipole m = I·(vector area); its far field has the same form as an electric dipole's.
- **M7 Magnetic fields in matter** [G 6; Z 13] ★
  - 7.1 Dia-, para- and ferromagnets; torque m×B; force ∇(m·B); orbital diamagnetism; M = magnetic moment per unit volume.
  - 7.2 Bound currents J_b = ∇×M and K_b = M×n̂.
  - 7.3 H = B/μ₀ − M with ∇×H = J_f; deceptive parallel (∇·H ≠ 0 in general); boundary conditions on H and B.
  - 7.4 χ_m and μ (magnetic susceptibility, permeability). ◇ Ferromagnetism: domains, hysteresis, Curie temperature.
- **M8 Synthesis: the static Maxwell equations and energy** ★ (catalog's "the Maxwell equations ... energy")
  - 8.1 The four static equations side by side (the div/curl table), with the Helmholtz theorem explaining why they plus boundary conditions are enough.
  - 8.2 One boundary-condition table for E, D, B, H, all derived from pillboxes and Amperian loops.
  - 8.3 Field energy; preview of magnetic energy.
  - 8.4 "What breaks when things move": a Faraday and displacement-current teaser that hands off to PHYS 4600.
  - ◇ GR on-ramp: Purcell & Morin-style "magnetism from relativity" lesson (see Key question 10).
- **Course boss battle: "The Boundary-Value Gauntlet".** Each stage is one method-choice problem (Gauss / superposition / images / separation of variables / multipole / dielectric BVP), scored on choosing the method *and* executing it. This mirrors the CUE's coupled method-plus-reasoning items (Key question 3).

**Mathematical skills.**
- Vector calculus in three coordinate systems; divergence and Stokes theorems.
- Dirac delta in 1D, 3D and curvilinear form.
- Taylor and binomial expansion for far fields (the 1/𝓇 expansion).
- Legendre polynomials: orthogonality, generating function, P₀–P₃ by heart.
- Separation of variables and Fourier series (orthogonality as a coefficient-extraction tool).
- Solid angle; symmetry arguments; ODE solving (Cauchy–Euler radial equation r^l, r^{−l−1}); order-of-magnitude and limit checks (r → ∞, R → 0).

**Prerequisites.**
- Calculus-based intro E&M: Coulomb, Gauss and Ampère at the integral level.
- Multivariable calculus (USU MATH 2210 is listed). The catalog title is not verified; it is very likely Multivariable Calculus.
- Linear algebra and/or ODEs (MATH 2250 or 2280).
- Intermediate mechanics (PHYS 3550): energy, rotation, oscillations.
- Modern physics (PHYS 2710): special-relativity basics, used again in E&M II.

**Foundations that short catalog descriptions leave implicit.** Each needs its own micro-lesson.
- Superposition and linearity, and its failure for energy.
- The separation vector 𝓇.
- Idealizations (infinite planes, lines, conductors) and why they work.
- Uniqueness theorems as the license for images and guess-and-check.
- Gauge-like freedom already present in statics: V → V + const, A → A + ∇λ.
- Fields as carriers of energy.
- Macroscopic averaging: why P, M, D, H exist.
- Charge conservation and the continuity equation.
- Unit systems: SI vs Gaussian (Griffiths App. C). Most GR texts use geometrized units, so flag this early.
- Reading the div/curl structure of a field directly from a picture.

## Key question 3 — E&M I (PHYS 3600): problem-solving techniques, problem archetypes, visualizations, depth and rigor, misconceptions

### Takeaway
The core skill of E&M I is *method selection*:
- superposition or direct integration;
- Gauss or Ampère (only with sufficient symmetry);
- potential first, then the field;
- images, separation of variables or multipoles for boundary-value problems.

The best-documented student errors are choosing the wrong method and fumbling the math of a method they did choose: δ-functions, pillboxes, implicit boundary conditions, infinite series. Interactive lessons should therefore make the *choice* explicit and visual before any computation.

### Cited Findings
- **Gauss's law (CUE, Pepper et al. 2010).** Of 325 students who took the CUE after E&M I, 33% did not recognize a radially symmetric problem as most easily solved with Gauss's law. Students who did choose it averaged 59% on explaining why and how it applied. — [Pepper et al., PERC 2010, "Our best juniors still struggle with Gauss's Law"](https://www.per-central.org/items/perc/1924.pdf)
- **Pillbox and flux errors (CU Boulder course materials).** Students forget the EA term on both faces of a pillbox where E ≠ 0 on one side, and so end up off by a factor of 2. They also compute zero flux through a region and wrongly conclude E = 0. — [CU Boulder, Electrostatics: challenges](https://physicscourses.colorado.edu/EducationIssues/Electrostatics/challenges.html)
- **Method choice (CUE coupled multiple-response item).** For a sphere with an off-center cavity, superposition is the easiest method. 45% chose it, 42% chose Gauss's law, and 6% chose integration. — [Validation and analysis of the coupled multiple response CUE, arXiv:1507.03936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.03936)
- **Curriculum sensitivity.** The CUE has been used to reveal differences between curricula. — [arXiv:1407.3400](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3400). ### Inferences
**Problem-solving technique ladder** (teach it explicitly, and revisit it in every module):
1. Exploit symmetry (Gauss, Ampère).
2. Superpose known solutions, including negative-charge "cavity" tricks.
3. Integrate Coulomb or Biot–Savart directly, setting up 𝓇 carefully.
4. Find V first, then E = −∇V.
5. Use uniqueness-licensed tricks (images; guess-and-check).
6. Separate variables with orthogonality.
7. Far-field multipole approximation.
8. Check limits and dimensions.

**Representative archetypes per module** (2–4 each):
- **M0 toolkit**
  - Verify the divergence theorem for a field in spherical coordinates over a hemisphere.
  - Evaluate ∫(r² + r·a)δ³(r − b)dτ, including the "point outside the volume → 0" trap.
  - Write ρ(r) for a ring, shell or line using δ-functions with correct units (PER-documented weakness).
  - Decide which of several fields could be electrostatic (∇×E = 0 test).
- **M1 field**
  - E on the axis of a ring or disk, with limits (z ≫ R recovers a point charge; R → ∞ recovers a plane).
  - Gauss's law for ρ = kr inside a sphere.
  - "Gauss is true but useless here": a finite line or a cube.
  - Superposition for a sphere with an off-center cavity (the CUE archetype).
- **M2 potential and energy**
  - V(r) for a uniformly charged sphere with reference at ∞.
  - Energy of that sphere two ways (½∫ρV and (ε₀/2)∫E²).
  - Find σ from the jump in ∂V/∂n.
  - Ranking task: which configurations need more work to assemble.
- **M3 conductors**
  - Concentric shells with given charges: induced charges on each surface, V(r).
  - Capacitance of spherical and coaxial capacitors.
  - Electrostatic pressure on a charged conducting sphere.
- **M4 boundary-value problems**
  - Point charge above a grounded plane: σ(x, y), total induced charge, force, and work to bring q in from ∞ (the factor-½ trap).
  - Grounded sphere image (q′ = −qR/a at R²/a).
  - Rectangular slot with V₀(y) on one side: Fourier sine series.
  - Sphere with σ₀cos θ, or a conducting sphere in a uniform field E₀; plus multipole moments of a discrete charge set.
- **M5 dielectrics**
  - Bound charges of a sphere with P = kr̂r.
  - Dielectric sphere in a uniform field: E inside = 3E₀/(ε_r + 2).
  - Capacitor half-filled in series vs. in parallel.
  - Force sucking a slab into a capacitor via dW/dx at constant Q vs. constant V.
- **M6 magnetostatics**
  - B at the center of a square loop and of a ring (Biot–Savart).
  - Thick wire with J = ks: Ampère inside and outside.
  - A for an infinite solenoid (inside and outside); B = ∇×A check.
  - Cycloid motion in crossed E and B.
- **M7 magnetic matter**
  - Uniformly magnetized sphere: K_b = M sin θ φ̂; B inside = (2/3)μ₀M.
  - Coaxial cable filled with a linear medium: H, B, M and bound currents.
  - Bar magnet: B vs. H inside (H points opposite to M).

**Distractor bank (E&M I).**
- PER-documented, cited above:
  - Using Gauss's law where superposition is easiest.
  - Not recognizing a symmetric problem as a Gauss's-law problem.
  - Pillbox factor-of-2 errors.
  - "Zero flux ⇒ zero E".
  - δ-functions written without units, or in Cartesian form for curvilinear geometry.
  - Separation of variables without the infinite sum, or missing implicit boundary conditions (finite at the origin, → 0 at ∞).
- Expert-anticipated, *not* PER-cited this session:
  - "V = 0 ⇒ E = 0"; "E = 0 ⇒ V = 0" (inside a conductor V is constant, not zero).
  - "The field in a cavity of a conductor is always zero", even with a charge inside the cavity.
  - Treating r̂ or φ̂ as constant inside an integral.
  - Swapping the source point r′ and the field point r in 𝓇.
  - Thinking the dipole moment never depends on the origin.
  - "Laplace solutions can have an interior maximum."
  - "Bound charge is fictitious."
  - "D depends only on free charge" (it does not, because ∇×D ≠ 0).
  - Calling H "the magnetic field" and expecting B to point opposite M inside a bar magnet.
  - "Magnetic forces do work" (the crane example).
  - "A is parallel to B."
  - Applying Ampère's law to a finite segment.
  - Sign or direction errors in μ₀K×n̂.
  - Adding the energies of superposed configurations.
  - Dropping the ½ in W = ½Σq_iV_i (double counting).

## Key question 4 — E&M II (PHYS 4600): major subjects, module tree, math skills, prerequisites, hidden foundations

### Takeaway
E&M II is the dynamic, relativistic half:
- induction and the full Maxwell equations;
- local conservation of energy and momentum (Poynting, Maxwell stress tensor);
- waves in vacuum, dielectrics and conductors (Fresnel, skin depth, dispersion), plus guided waves;
- potentials and gauge freedom, retarded and Liénard–Wiechert potentials;
- radiation (dipole, Larmor);
- the covariant formulation (F^{μν}, four-potential, Lorenz gauge).

This is the single most important bridge course into GR. USU's catalog lists every one of these strands. Waveguides and radiation reaction are where instructors most often trim.

### Inferences
**Module tree for E&M II.** ★ = core, ◇ = enrichment, [G]/[Z] as before.

- **M0 Bridge: statics in field language** ★. A five-lesson recap: the div/curl table, the boundary-condition table, V and A, energy in fields, and the special-relativity refresher hooks.
- **M1 Electrodynamics** [G 7; Z 14–15] ★
  - 1.1 Ohm's law J = σE (σ = conductivity); resistance; Joule heating; why the steady field in a wire needs surface charges.
  - 1.2 EMF ℰ = ∮f·dl; motional EMF and the flux rule. "Who does the work?" (the magnetic force does not).
  - 1.3 Faraday's law: ∮E·dl = −dΦ_B/dt and ∇×E = −∂B/∂t; Lenz's law; induced E in symmetric geometries (the "Ampère-like" trick); the quasistatic regime and its limits.
  - 1.4 Inductance: mutual inductance via the Neumann formula (M₁₂ = M₂₁); self-inductance; back EMF; LR transients.
  - 1.5 Magnetic energy: W = ½LI² = (1/2μ₀)∫B² dτ.
  - 1.6 Maxwell's fix.
    - ∇·(∇×B) = 0 conflicts with ∇·J ≠ 0, so Maxwell adds a displacement current ε₀∂E/∂t.
    - The full equations.
    - Magnetic-monopole symmetry.
    - The equations in matter (D, H, ρ_f, J_f; polarization current ∂P/∂t).
    - General boundary conditions.
- **M2 Conservation laws** [G 8] ★
  - 2.1 Local charge conservation (the continuity equation as the *template* for every conservation law).
  - 2.2 Poynting's theorem.
    - dW/dt = −d/dt∫u dτ − ∮S·da, with u = ½(ε₀E² + B²/μ₀) and S = (1/μ₀)E×B.
    - Showcase: energy enters a resistor through its *sides*.
    - Showcase: the charging capacitor.
  - 2.3 Momentum.
    - Newton's third law appears to fail for fields.
    - Maxwell stress tensor T_ij (i, j = x, y, z): force = ∮T·da − ε₀μ₀(d/dt)∫S dτ.
    - Field momentum density g = ε₀μ₀S; radiation pressure.
  - 2.4 Field angular momentum ℓ = r×g; ◇ the Feynman disk paradox and hidden momentum.
  - 2.5 "Magnetic forces do no work" [G 8.3] and its apparent counter-examples.
- **M3 Electromagnetic waves** [G 9; Z 16–19] ★
  - 3.1 Waves in 1D.
    - The wave equation; sinusoidal waves.
    - Complex notation f̃ = Ãe^{i(kz−ωt)}; "the real part is physical".
    - Reflection and transmission at a string junction (an impedance analogue).
    - Linear, circular and elliptical polarization.
  - 3.2 Vacuum waves.
    - Wave equations for E and B; c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀).
    - Plane waves are transverse, with B = (1/c)k̂×E in phase.
    - Energy and momentum: S = cu k̂; intensity I = ⟨S⟩ (time average); radiation pressure I/c (absorbed) or 2I/c (reflected).
  - 3.3 Waves in linear media.
    - v = c/n with n ≈ √(ε_rμ_r).
    - Normal incidence: R and T from n₁ and n₂.
    - Oblique incidence: the three laws (same frequency; reflection angle; Snell); the Fresnel equations; Brewster's angle.
    - ◇ Total internal reflection and evanescent waves.
  - 3.4 Absorption and dispersion.
    - Conductors: free charge decays with τ = ε/σ; complex wave number k̃ = k + iκ; skin depth d = 1/κ; B lags E; metals reflect.
    - Frequency-dependent permittivity from the Lorentz (damped bound-electron) oscillator: complex ε, absorption coefficient, anomalous dispersion, Cauchy's formula.
    - ◇ Plasma frequency. ◇ Kramers–Kronig relations (causality ⇒ dispersion).
  - 3.5 Guided waves [G 9.5].
    - Wall boundary conditions: E∥ = 0, B⊥ = 0.
    - TE and TM modes; TE_mn in a rectangular guide with cutoff ω_mn = cπ√((m/a)² + (n/b)²).
    - Phase velocity > c while group velocity < c; no TEM mode in a hollow guide.
    - TEM mode of a coaxial line.
    - ◇ Cavity resonators (Zangwill's "confined waves").
- **M4 Potentials and fields** [G 10; Z 15, 20, 23] ★
  - 4.1 E = −∇V − ∂A/∂t and B = ∇×A turn Maxwell into two coupled equations for V and A.
  - 4.2 Gauge transformations: A → A + ∇λ, V → V − ∂λ/∂t (λ = any scalar function, the "gauge function").
    - Coulomb gauge ∇·A = 0: V is instantaneous, yet E is still causal.
    - Lorenz gauge ∇·A = −μ₀ε₀∂V/∂t gives decoupled wave equations □²V = −ρ/ε₀ and □²A = −μ₀J. Here □² is the d'Alembertian, "the wave operator" ∇² − (1/c²)∂²/∂t².
  - 4.3 Lorentz force in potential form; canonical momentum p + qA (hands off to minimal coupling in QM I).
  - 4.4 Retarded time t_r = t − 𝓇/c; retarded potentials; Jefimenko's equations (the time-dependent Coulomb and Biot–Savart laws).
  - 4.5 Liénard–Wiechert potentials, with the factor 1/(1 − 𝓇̂·v/c).
    - The fields split into a velocity ("generalized Coulomb", ∝ 1/𝓇²) part and an acceleration ("radiation", ∝ 1/𝓇) part.
    - A uniformly moving charge's E points from its *present* position, flattened into a "pancake" at high speed.
- **M5 Radiation** [G 11; Z 20–21] ★
  - 5.1 Radiation = power that reaches ∞, so only 1/r fields count. Near, intermediate and radiation zones.
  - 5.2 Electric dipole radiation.
    - Approximations d ≪ λ ≪ r.
    - ⟨S⟩ ∝ sin²θ/r² (doughnut pattern, nothing along the axis).
    - ⟨P⟩ = μ₀p₀²ω⁴/(12πc). The ω⁴ dependence explains why the sky is blue.
  - 5.3 Magnetic dipole radiation, suppressed by (ωb/c)² (b = loop radius).
  - 5.4 Arbitrary sources; the multipole hierarchy. ◇ Electric quadrupole radiation, the direct GW analogue.
  - 5.5 Point charges.
    - Larmor P = μ₀q²a²/(6πc).
    - Liénard's relativistic generalization (γ⁶ for collinear acceleration).
    - Angular pattern and relativistic beaming; bremsstrahlung; ◇ synchrotron radiation.
  - 5.6 ◇ Radiation reaction (Abraham–Lorentz F = μ₀q²ȧ/(6πc)); runaway solutions and preacceleration.
  - 5.7 ◇ Scattering (Thomson, Rayleigh) and ◇ antennas (half-wave antenna, radiation resistance).
- **M6 Relativistic electrodynamics** [G 12; Z 22–24] ★
  - 6.1 Special-relativity review: postulates; relativity of simultaneity; Lorentz transformations; the invariant interval; four-vectors; spacetime diagrams.
  - 6.2 Relativistic mechanics: proper time τ; proper velocity η^μ; four-momentum p^μ; E² − p²c² = m²c⁴; collisions; the Minkowski force K^μ.
  - 6.3 Magnetism as a relativistic phenomenon (the moving-wire argument; Purcell & Morin's spine).
  - 6.4 Field transformations: E∥ and B∥ unchanged; E⊥′ = γ(E + v×B)⊥; B⊥′ = γ(B − v×E/c²)⊥. Invariants E·B and E² − c²B².
  - 6.5 The field tensor F^{μν} (an antisymmetric 4×4 array whose six independent entries are E/c and B) and its dual G^{μν}. Upper vs. lower indices; the metric η_{μν}.
  - 6.6 Covariant Maxwell: ∂_νF^{μν} = μ₀J^μ and ∂_νG^{μν} = 0, with J^μ = (cρ, J) and ∂_μJ^μ = 0. Lorentz force K^μ = qη_νF^{μν}.
  - 6.7 Four-potential A^μ = (V/c, A); F^{μν} = ∂^μA^ν − ∂^νA^μ; Lorenz gauge ∂_μA^μ = 0 ⇒ □A^μ = −μ₀J^μ.
  - 6.8 ◇ EM stress-energy tensor T^{μν} (unites u, S/c and the Maxwell stress; traceless). This is the GR bridge (Key question 10).
  - 6.9 ◇ Lagrangian formulation: particle action; field Lagrangian density −(1/4μ₀)F_{μν}F^{μν}. This is the QFT bridge (Zangwill ch. 24).

  3. Waves at interfaces and in conductors.
  4. Gauge choice and retarded fields.
