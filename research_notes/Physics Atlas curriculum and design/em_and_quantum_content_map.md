## Key question 1 — Course identity, and how the Griffiths chapters split between E&M I and II (all four courses)

### Takeaway
USU's current catalog wording maps PHYS 3600 onto Griffiths ch. 1–6 (statics plus materials) and PHYS 4600 onto ch. 7–12 (induction through relativistic electrodynamics). That is the most common national split. The variants are 1–5 | 6–12, and E&M II courses that cut parts of ch. 8 and 11. Third-party listings describe PHYS 4700 (QM I) as reaching angular momentum, spin and perturbation theory. No official USU description of PHYS 4710 (QM II) was found, and listings disagree on the numbering.

Topics include the Maxwell equations, Coulomb's and Biot-Savart laws, scalar and vector potentials, boundary conditions, multipole expansions, and energy." Prerequisites: PHYS 2710, PHYS 3550, MATH 2210, and MATH 2250 or MATH 2280. — [USU catalog, PHYS 3600](https://usu.catalog.acalog.com/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=39&coid=330709)

Prerequisites: PHYS 3600 and PHYS 3750. — [USU catalog, PHYS 4600](https://catalog.usu.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=40&coid=338889)

### Inferences
- **Mapping USU onto Griffiths E&M.**
  - PHYS 3600 ≈ Griffiths ch. 1–6. "Response of materials ... to electric and magnetic fields" brings in ch. 4 and 6. Expect "the Maxwell equations" there to mean the static div/curl equations, with at most a preview of the full set.
  - PHYS 4600 ≈ ch. 7–12. Catalog phrase → chapter: "electromagnetic induction" → ch. 7; "conservation laws with fields" → ch. 8; "electromagnetic waves" and "dynamic response of materials" → ch. 9 (9.3–9.4); "electromagnetic potentials" → ch. 10; "radiation" → ch. 11; "relativistic description" → ch. 12, especially 12.3.
  - Build the Atlas on that 1–6 | 7–12 split. Optionally add a "bridge" module at the start of E&M II that recaps statics in the language of fields and potentials.
- **PHYS 3750 is a prerequisite for 4600.** That puts Fourier and wave-equation skills before E&M II at USU, so the E&M II waves module can assume them but should include a quick-review lesson.
- **Perturbation theory sits in QM I at USU.** PHYS 4700's listed content ends with "perturbation theory and applications", so time-independent perturbation theory belongs to the Atlas QM I. That leaves QM II for identical particles, the other approximation methods, scattering and dynamics, which is the usual Griffiths Part II split (see Key question 8).
- **Recommended Atlas course identity strings.** Use the course numbers and names given in the brief (PHYS 3600 Electromagnetism I, PHYS 4600 Electromagnetism II, PHYS 4700 Quantum Mechanics I, PHYS 4710 Quantum Mechanics II). Credit hours and descriptions come from the catalog entries above. PHYS 4710 wording is still pending the catalog verifier.
